# TestRoom: 100 room themes

The 104-room loop cut to 100 (50 down, 50 up), upgraded with the weird pass from room_themes_40_weird.md, and given a new Flip column. Neither older file is changed. Every room of the 40-room weird pass appears here in its weird form, at the place its "(was N)" number has in the 104-room plan. In the Theme column, "(was N)" is the room's number in room_themes_104.md, ⚑ marks a room carried over from the weird pass, + marks a room that gets a new touch of the same spirit in this pass, and * still marks a surprise room.

What is new since the 40-room build: a flip used to be a tone step only, and was too easy to miss. Now a flip does three things at once, while the object is unseen: the tone step, a colour tint, and a move to a second, fixed place.

## Tint system

**The idea in one line.** Unpainted is pencil; painted is ink. Every unpainted object is drawn in neutral graphite grey, with no colour at all. When it flips it is inked in its room's colour, still wet. So the question "has this flipped?" is always the same question: "is it grey or is it coloured?", in every room and at every tone.

**1. Where the hue comes from.** The hue wheel walks once round the loop and never goes backward. Each act owns an arc of the wheel, and each room takes the next small step along it, so a room is always close to its neighbours and an act has a colour you can name.

- The descent runs cool to hot, blue to ember: the further down, the redder the ink. The ascent runs ember through coral and green back to clear blue, so room 100's ink blue sits next to room 1's cobalt and the loop closes in colour as well as in tone.
- **Gold is skipped.** The wheel jumps from burnt orange (68) straight to lichen green (69), across the yellow-gold arc. Gold belongs to the pickup cube, which stays the only gold thing in the walk and never tints, flips or moves by itself.
- An echo room never has its original's hue. Dinner is indigo, Breakfast is viridian; the Pencil Forest is cobalt, the sharpened one cerulean. The layout says "you have been here"; the colour says "not like this".
- Hexes in this file are key colours at one fixed lightness (OKLCH L 0.66, C 0.15, hue from the Flip parameters table) so they can be compared. They are what goes into `_RoomColor`; the shader makes the lighter and darker versions below from them.

**2. What tints.** Objects, fully: body, line work and rim. Walls, floors and ceilings keep neutral paper at every tone; only their contour lines take the room's ink, at about one third of object strength, so a flipped panel reads as "inked" next to an unflipped one while objects stay the only saturated things in view. The floor feature and ceiling feature take it at two thirds and flip with their surface. One float, `_SurfaceTint` (default 0.33), sets this; at 0 the rule is "items only", exactly as Cor first said it. Fog and hallways never tint.

**3. How strong, at each tone.** The paper cannot carry colour at the white end and the black end, so the tint moves from the lines, to the body, to the lines and rim.

- Line weight goes up by about a quarter on a painted object, at every tone: inked lines are a little fatter than pencil lines.
- At both ends the painted lines also change in lightness, not only in hue (darker on white, brighter on black), so the flip still reads for anyone who sees hue poorly.
- The polarity of the line work (dark on light, or pale on dark) changes at mid grey, in Half Past Grey (24) on the way down and Half Past Grey, Returning (76) on the way up. Those two rooms have a lighter and a darker half, so they show both at once.

**4. With the ink pattern and the iridescence.** The swirl and star pattern is unchanged in shape. Unpainted it is graphite; painted it is the room's ink. The thin-film sheen stays, but is pulled harder toward the room hue (`lerp(pal, _RoomColor, 0.7)` instead of 0.5), so it shimmers within the room's colour rather than through the whole rainbow, and its strength follows darkness: off in the pale rooms, where it never read, and full from the dark greys down. The painted mix for any room becomes: 70% the room's neutral tone, 20% tint, 10% pale line and rim. Optional, and cheap: a just-flipped object is glossier for its first few seconds in view (`_Wet` falling from 1 to 0), so fresh ink looks wet and then settles.

**5. The glance test.** From the far doorway, squinting, a painted object must differ from an unpainted copy of itself in overall colour by about ΔE 0.06 in OKLab, and it must also not be where it was. If a room fails the first half, raise line weight and rim before raising saturation.

**6. Things that break the rule, on purpose.**

- Never takes the ink: the eraser slabs (2), the dry black puddle (7), the white petal (97), and the cube everywhere.
- Has colour before any flip: the rainbow rim of the Cap Cloud (27).
- Takes more than its room's ink: the Grey Rainbow (71), seven bands in seven inks, and the ring of discs in The Whole Walk (98), a hundred inks, one for each room. The rainbow is the payoff; the ring is the map.
- Takes another room's ink: the chair and moon inside the wide bell (60) and the paper sheet on the washing line (74) go cobalt, room 1's colour.
- Rooms 50 and 51 are black on black and tint least: one ember hairline per object.

## Flip moves

Each object has exactly two places: where it stands unpainted (pose A) and where it stands painted (pose B). Both are authored by hand and fixed. Nothing is ever placed at random, and a flip always takes an object from A to B in one unseen step, together with its tone and its tint.

- **Down unsettles, up settles.** In the descent, pose B is the wrong one: things drift, tilt, turn away, slip off their marks. In the ascent, pose B is the right one: things square up, pull in, face the exit. Wherever an ascent room echoes a descent room, its pose A is the mess the descent left (the echo's unpainted layout is its original's painted layout), so painting a room on the way up is tidying it.
- **Size.** Slides of 0.2 to 1.2 m, turns, a change of height, or a change of shape (an umbrella opens, a clam gapes). Never more than 1.5 m.
- **Anchors never move:** the cube and whatever it rests on, anything you can stand on, anything that goes through a wall, floor or ceiling, wall-mounted giants, and the floor and ceiling features. Anchors still take the ink.
- **Clear space.** No pose A or pose B, and no path between them, may touch a doorway's keep-out (the doorway's width plus 0.5 m, 1.2 m deep into the room) or the cube's 1.5 m circle.
- **Nothing inside anything.** Each mover owns a private envelope, the space of its pose A and pose B together. Envelopes of different objects do not overlap, so any mix of flipped and unflipped objects is safe. Objects that trade or share space (coats along one rail, the settings on a table) flip as one unit.
- **Unseen behaviours are a separate thing.** The weird pass's repeating behaviours (boats creeping, the chair in 50, the hand in 56) carry on as before. A flip happens once per object; a behaviour repeats. Where one object has both, the row says which wins.
- **Quiet rooms flip quietly.** Rooms 1, 45, 49, 50, 79, 90, 97 and 100 keep their moves to a turn or a small slide.

## The arc

One closed walk: down from bright paper to deep night (rooms 1-50), then back up through a dawn (51-100), so the doorway out of 100 opens onto room 1 with no cut. The descent is a first draft, and it is where the dream goes wrong. The ascent visits the same five worlds in reverse order, changed by the trip: things are lifted, lit, mended, set afloat or answered. The wrongness is answered too, so the ascent is calmer than the descent on purpose.

**Tone.** With t = 0 white and 1 black: step s(n) = (n-1)/49 for rooms 1-50 and (100-n)/49 for rooms 51-100, and painted tone t(n) = 1 - (1 - s)^1.08, a very gentle ease-out. Rooms 50 and 51 are both black, 100 and 1 both white, and the walk crosses mid grey at room 24 going down (0.50) and between 76 and 77 coming up (0.52, 0.50). The unpainted default lags two rooms behind, as in the 40-room build: default(n) = t(n-2), wrapping at the join. Going down (3-50) painting darkens; coming up (52-100, and on into room 1) it brightens. Rooms 2 and 51 sit against the grain. At 100 rooms a two-room lag is only a 4% tone step, which is why tint and move now carry the flip. A four-room lag (8%) is worth trying: it would make every unpainted room exactly the tone of the last room on its footprint.

**Rules for every room**

- Objects use the shared swirl/star ink pattern, a few shades darker than the walls. In the darkest rooms (about 43-58) they read by pale line work and rim only. In the last white rooms the ink thins instead: objects are found by outline.
- No other cube-like shapes anywhere: boxes are always clearly flat, long or tall, so the pickup cube stays the only cube. Miniature rooms (12, 40) never contain a miniature cube; at most a hair-thin beam line.
- The cube sits in a 1.5 m clear circle with nothing taller than 1 m beside it, at 0.5 to 1.2 m height. Its beam must be visible from both doorways. Nothing that moves, follows, hangs or flips may enter the circle.
- Rooms n and n+4 share a footprint, across the join too (100 shares with 4, 97 with 1). An "Echo" reuses the layout of an earlier room on the same footprint; an echo is never a copy: something in it has been mended, moved or finished.
- Echo families, all on one footprint each: 1 > 45. 3 > 95. 4 > 68. 5 > 93. 8 > 40 > 96. 10 > 90. 11 > 19 > 87. 12 > 36 > 64. 16 > 88. 18 > 82. 20 > 84. 24 > 76. 25 > 73. 27 > 71. 32 > 80. 37 > 65. 41 > 61. 42 > 62. 43 > 55. 44 > 52. 46 > 54. 49 > 53, 77 and 97, with 89 on the sun's footprint on purpose. 56 stands on 48's footprint. The sun now shares room 1's footprint, which room 97 uses.
- One floor feature and one ceiling feature per room, flat or small, outside the cube's clear circle, one merged mesh each. Floors and ceilings only change when fully out of view.
- Repeated small pieces (rain, books, chain links, miniature rooms) are one merged mesh and count as one object.
- Surprise rooms are marked with a star: 19, 24, 25, 27, 42, 45, 50 on the way down; 51, 62, 71, 73, 76, 78, 90, 98 on the way up.
- **Through the bounds.** Where a row says an object goes "through" a wall, floor or ceiling, it simply intersects it, like 48's star: no hole is cut and nothing shows on the far side. One such object per room at most.
- **Unseen behaviours.** A few objects also move, repeatedly, while unseen: a snap between fixed poses or a small step, per player, locally. Nothing ever moves fast in view. One unseen behaviour per room at most, and none in rooms 1, 48, 49, 90, 97 and 100.
- **The paper misbehaves twice only.** The contour pattern itself moves in two rooms: it breathes with the sleeper in 10, and creeps toward the sun's corner in 49.
- **The ascent answers the wrongness.** The endless table gets ends (87), the watched clocks run (82), the sleeper has got up (90), the eye is asleep (61), the kite shadow lets go of you (80), and the grey rainbow is allowed its colours (71).

**Threads**

- The sun. Room 49's giant returns on the same footprint three times, higher and smaller: under the floor (53), behind cloud (77), a pale disc on the wall (97). The flowers in 89 face its corner. Its ash petals fall sideways in 49, rise in 53, and one lies white on a bench in 97. New in this plan: that footprint is also room 1's, and the risen sun is exactly the size of room 1's wall moon.
- The paper boat that faced back toward room 1 (room 4) leaves the fleet in 68 and is found in 99. It is the one object that never moves in any flip.
- The small thirteenth chair (11, 19) gets a coat in 86 and a cushion in 87.
- The carpet scroll (8), torn in 40, is stitched whole in 96.
- The chair from room 1 turns toward the moon when room 1 flips, faces it in 45, is alone in 50, and is a constellation in 56. Its shadow in 45 has somebody sitting in it.
- The hairline that ends room 50 starts room 51, and the last room (100) is room 1 before it was drawn.
- The hand you never see. A pencil line grows behind you in 3, a chalk line in 51, and in 56 you finally see the worker, from the knees down. In 95 the line is finished.
- The sheet that leans on nothing (1) casts no shadow in 45, and in 100 you see the construction line it was leaning on.
- Things out of place. The hulls over 35 are room 4's fleet seen from below; one sits on the ceiling of 42; the slings in 63 bring up a chair, a clock and a slipper from the house; an umbrella from 23 has taken root in 72; the dust sheets of 20 hang washed in 74.
- New: the poses themselves are a thread. Room 1's painted layout is room 45's unpainted one; room 100's painted layout is room 1's unpainted one; every descent mess is where its ascent echo starts.

**The turn (50 and 51).** Both rooms are black. Room 50's exit doorway is as dark as the room, and its trim is one pale hairline, the last drawn line of the descent. The line carries on along the hallway floor into 51, where it becomes the first mark of the climb, and is still being drawn.

## Build notes

- **One flip, three changes.** Tone, tint and pose change in the same frame, by the same unseen test the paint already uses. Tint needs no new data per object: `_Painted` already switches the look, and `_RoomColor` already exists; unpainted simply uses zero chroma. Pose needs one stored offset per mover (position, rotation, and for a few a scale or a second mesh).
- **Per-room unseen behaviour (unchanged from the weird pass, renumbered):** 3 line grows. 4 boats creep. 11 a chair pulls out. 18 clocks freeze when seen. 10 sleeper turns over. 27 cloudlets follow. 24 one extra piece toggles. 22 rain steps down. 32 one shadow moves under you. 41 lidded ring changes wall. 42 domes sag (3 steps). 50 chair comes closer. 12 exit doorway shows an area copy of 12 (static). 51 star gains strokes, chalk moves. 56 hand lowers. 89 one flower turns. 79 the cloud drifts toward the nearest player (in view, slow).

- **Multi-ink objects** (71, 98) and the borrowed-ink props (60, 74) need a per-object or per-vertex colour instead of the room's `_RoomColor`. 98's ring is one mesh: put the hundred key colours in vertex colour and flip discs by a per-disc mask.
